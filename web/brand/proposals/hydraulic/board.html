Direction

Silicon · the hour is the product

SILICON

The thing traded is one hour of a real chip, so the chip is the hero: drawn like a museum specimen, from the heatsink that gives the company its name down to the die, and named in plain text, never in anyone's trade dress.

Every order is a die on a wafer. A round is a wafer under test: sealed grey, probed and revealed in the two colours of the two sides, then the filled ones lit at one price, which turns the auction into an image instead of a chart.

Everything else is quiet and exact: a neutral cleanroom ground, one dot-matrix face for numbers you should remember, a monospace spec sheet for every fact, and colour spent only on the two groups of people the market serves.

Why the first screen is a wafer

  1. Atmosphere first. A great wafer fills the right of the screen, about 1,100 dies at desktop. Nothing to read; it is simply alive.
  2. And it is true. Every die is an order in a simulated sealed round. Sealed, they are grey. A probe sweeps the wafer and reveals each one in its side's colour. Then the clearing rule of SPEC section 7 is run on that book, and only the filled dies light up, all at one price. A new book is drawn each round, so it never repeats.
  3. The people are the colour. Blue is everyone who needs compute; hatched gold is everyone who owns it. Nothing else on the site uses colour.
  4. The claim, not the mechanism. "Take the heat out of your GPU bill." Dry, about the person paying, and the name explains itself. The explanation is one scroll down, as an object you can take apart.
  5. Honest and light. The index plate says Sample; the round caption says Simulated. One canvas, no library, pixel ratio capped at 2, paused off screen and in hidden tabs. Reduced motion paints one cleared wafer and stops.

Why blue and gold. They sit at opposite ends of the blue–yellow axis that protan and deutan readers keep, and they differ in lightness, so the two sides survive every common colour-vision deficiency and greyscale print. They also come from the object: the deep blue of a lithography exposure and the gold of a package's contacts. Gold is never shown without a second cue (hatching, a dashed line, the word SHORT), blue is always solid. No red–green pair, no purple gradient, no glow.
