01 · Direction

Swarm.

This is a market between people who need GPU time, increasingly their AI agents, and people who own the GPUs, so the brand is populated by the thing they trade: small chip-people, a package with pins for legs and a face on the heat spreader, ultramarine when they buy and amber, wearing a heatsink, when they sell. The market is shown as a shift of work they do together on a data-hall floor: they bring sealed bids, open them at once, rack them by price and light the one slot where both sides meet. The wit is in behaviour (timing, glances, a late arrival turned away) and the craft is in the drawing, so the same kit carries a landing page, a spec sheet and a dense order book.

The one feelingA floor full of busy little machines, and every one of them got a fair price.

The wordmark is set as "Weir", and nothing here depends on the name: neither mark is built from a letter. The house character is a heatsink because it is the most recognisable part of a GPU, a finned creature who keeps the floor cool while the chip-people trade. No water, no flames, no thermometers, no red-to-blue gradients: cool, precise, a little dry.

  • Side is told twice, by colour and by silhouette (fins), so it survives colour blindness and greyscale. At flock size, where fins vanish, sellers are drawn hollow.
  • Expression lives in the eyes (two 3.2 x 6.6 capsules) and in motion. Pins are legs and arms; there is no mouth.
  • A bid is a sled: sealed under a locked cover, then opened to show its price per GPU-hour.

A 22 x 13 base with 3.6-unit corners stands on two pin feet; seven fins, 1.8 wide on a 3.1 pitch, rise only 4.8 to 7.8 units out of it with rounded tops on a shallow arc, so the crest reads as a comb, a mohawk or a heatsink, never as fingers, a bar chart or a signal icon. 03 · The hero

A statement over a living flock, then the floor.

The first screen is atmosphere and a claim: a few hundred chip-agents, buyers as solid ultramarine squares and sellers as hollow amber ones, wheel freely and every eighteen seconds or so settle on one price line, then let go. Directly under it, Fig. 1 is a data-hall floor in a pill-shaped window where one round is worked as a shift: sealed bids in, open at once, rack by price, one slot lights, the rest carry their bids home. It loops with new prices, arrival orders and small accidents.

Fig. 1, second by second
  1. 0.0 to 2.8 s · Bring. Agents walk in from both ends carrying sled-shaped bids under locked covers. Each bid locks dollars; side, size and price stay hidden.
  2. 2.8 s · Open. Every cover pops at the same moment. A late agent gets turned away: the round is sealed.
  3. 3.4 s · Line up. They re-sort by price, the keenest buyers and the cheapest sellers nearest the rack.
  4. 4.5 s · Rack. Each bid is tossed into the slot for its price: buyers' on the left of the spine, sellers' on the right.
  5. 7.0 s · Scan. A scanner climbs the rack counting buyers at or above and sellers at or below, overshoots once and settles where the most GPU-hours match.
  6. 8.4 s · One price. That slot lights. Filled bids get a tick and stay racked; every filled agent pays that one price. The rest take their bids home.
  7. All along · the house heatsink. The brand character sits on top of the rack keeping the floor cool. It turns the late agent away, watches the scan, and says "Cool." when the price lands. That is the whole joke, every round.

Clearing is computed in the page with the rule in SPEC section 7 each loop; prices are examples and every bid is 64 GPU-h.

05 · Tokens

Two colours carry meaning. Everything else stays out of their way.

Ultramarine against amber is the one pair that stays apart for every common colour-vision type: it differs in hue for deutan and protan readers and in lightness for everyone (relative luminance about 0.10 against 0.50), and every agent repeats the side in its silhouette. Bricolage Grotesque gives headlines a warm, slightly irregular voice; Instrument Sans keeps text plain; Martian Mono, narrowed to 87.5%, makes every figure line up like a spec sheet.

Contrast is computed in the page against the ground of the same theme. Fills marked "shape only" are never used for text; their text twin is the "-ink" token.
